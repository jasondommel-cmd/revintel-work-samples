01

The Premise

Every quarter, GTM teams spend heavily to generate new demand. Meanwhile, the clearest buying signals a company will ever get are sitting, unread, inside the calls its own reps already had.

Buyers tell you they're ready. They just tell you on a Tuesday call that no one ever listens to again.
The thesis behind the audit

The company had just released "Core" — its next-generation AI product. The default GTM instinct was to build outbound campaigns and generate fresh demand for it. My argument was different: with 31,000 recorded sales and customer conversations in the conversational-intelligence platform, the accounts most ready to buy Core had almost certainly already described their own need for it — in discovery calls, QBRs, and support escalations that predated the product's launch.

The problem was never a data problem. It was a listening-at-scale problem. No human team can re-read 31,000 transcripts, hold four distinct buying signals in mind at once, and flag only the accounts where all four co-occur. That is exactly the kind of pattern recognition AI agents are built for.

02

Method: Agents That Actually Read

Proven on a sample, scaled on the corpus

This wasn't keyword search. Keyword search finds the word "integration"; it can't tell the difference between a buyer describing a painful gap and a rep name-dropping a feature. The audit used AI agents to read for meaning and intent — the discipline of conversational intelligence — then score each account against a four-signal buying pattern.

Step 1 · Prove the read

Validate on a call cluster

Before trusting anything at scale, I ran the extraction against a controlled batch of transcripts and hand-checked it — confirming the agents surfaced real, in-context pain and intent rather than surface keyword hits, and stayed close to what buyers actually said.

Stack: Claude agents over raw transcript exports, human-validated
Step 2 · Scale the read

Batch the full 31K corpus

With the method validated, extraction moved to Databricks — batch-processing all 31,000 transcripts, classifying each against the four signals, and writing structured, queryable results back to the warehouse instead of trapping them in prose.

Stack: Databricks for scale · structured signal scores per account
Step 3 · Qualify the pattern

Find the four-signal overlap

The unlock wasn't any single signal — it was co-occurrence. Accounts were qualified only where all four independent buying signals appeared in the same relationship, turning a noisy corpus into a short, ranked, sales-ready list.

Output: 109 accounts where all four signals stack

Division of labor by design: Databricks does what it's best at — heavy, repeatable batch classification across the whole corpus — while Claude agents do the interpretive, in-context reading and the iterative cluster analysis. Neither tool alone gets you to a defensible list of 109.

03

The Four Buyer Signals

In the buyer's language

Each signal is a thing a buyer says when they're unknowingly describing a fit for Core. Individually, each is common and easy to dismiss. The insight was that their intersection is rare — and predictive. These map to the same buyer-language patterns that surfaced repeatedly in the transcript analysis: manual-processing pain, exception/accuracy friction, capacity limits, and stalled evaluations.

Signal 01 · Manual-effort pain

"We're still doing this by hand"

The account explicitly describes manual, high-labor work that Core is designed to eliminate — the buyer naming the cost of the status quo without being prompted.

Heard as: hours per document, headcount tied to data entry, "we just throw people at it."
Signal 02 · Accuracy & exception friction

"The errors are killing us"

The buyer raises accuracy problems, exception handling, or rework loops — the precise failure mode Core's AI directly addresses, described as an active operational wound.

Heard as: matching failures, rework queues, "we catch it downstream, if we catch it."
Signal 03 · Scale & capacity ceiling

"We can't grow like this"

Volume is outrunning the current process. The buyer frames a growth or capacity constraint — the strategic trigger that turns a nice-to-have into a funded priority.

Heard as: volume spikes, seasonal overload, "we'd need to hire three more people to keep up."
Signal 04 · Stalled or unresolved evaluation

"We looked at this and never finished"

An earlier evaluation stalled, or a need was raised and never closed — a warm, half-open door the AI found in old calls that the current team had forgotten was ever ajar.

Heard as: prior POC that fizzled, "we shelved it," a champion who went quiet.

The "Core Readiness" Play

The trigger
An in-flight account whose own recorded conversations contain all four buying signals — manual pain, accuracy friction, a capacity ceiling, and an unresolved earlier evaluation. The buyer has already made the case for Core, out loud, on a call.
The rep's opening
Not a cold pitch — a callback. "Last quarter your team mentioned X was still manual and the errors were creating rework. We built something for exactly that." The rep walks in already knowing the pain, in the buyer's own words.
Why it converts
It inverts the burden of discovery. The account is pre-qualified on need, timing, and fit before the first dial — the highest-intent list the GTM team could possibly work, assembled from demand that already existed.
05

From Signals to Sales Plays

Illustrative scenarios

The 109-account list was the headline, but the audit also produced a reusable play library: because every account carried its signal profile, the same conversational-intelligence engine could route each one to the right motion instead of one generic sequence. Below are illustrative plays the signal architecture unlocks.

Play A · All four signals

Core Readiness callback

Highest-intent motion. Executive-led callback referencing the buyer's own recorded pain. Fast-tracked to a tailored Core demo built from the account's stated needs.

Priority: immediate · 109 accounts
Play B · Pain + accuracy, no capacity signal

Value-reframe nurture

Real pain but no stated growth pressure yet. Nurture that reframes accuracy cost as a scaling risk — moving the account toward the capacity trigger that makes Core urgent.

Priority: medium · surfaced as a distinct tier
Play C · Stalled evaluation only

Re-open the door

A prior evaluation that fizzled. Light-touch, champion-focused re-engagement that references what stalled it and what's changed — recovering pipeline everyone had written off.

Priority: recovery · win-back motion

The plays and their tiering are illustrative of what the four-signal architecture enables. The underlying audit, the 109 qualified accounts, the $50K average ACV, and the $5.45M surfaced pipeline are the audit's actual output.

What this demonstrates

This is demand discovery — finding pipeline that already exists, not manufacturing it.

Most GTM leaders answer a pipeline gap by spending more on demand generation. This work answered it by mining the company's own conversational intelligence with AI agents — turning 31,000 unread transcripts into a ranked, sales-ready list worth $5.45M, and a reusable play library that routes each account to the right motion. It pairs three things a revenue org rarely gets in one person: fluency in the AI and data tooling (Claude agents, Databricks), a genuine grasp of buyer and customer language, and the operational discipline to turn a model output into plays a sales team will actually run.
