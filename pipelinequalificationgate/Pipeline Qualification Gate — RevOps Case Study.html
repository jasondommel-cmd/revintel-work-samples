01

The Hypothesis

Leadership was forecasting off a pipeline that looked healthy on face value — roughly $10M of open new-logo ARR. My hypothesis: most of it wasn't qualified opportunity at all, just hot prospects with an opportunity record attached. Rather than argue it, I tested it.

The contact-level join told the story immediately: 96% of pre-Develop deals failed a basic qualification bar, 89% were single-threaded, and the problem wasn't confined to early stage — 24 of 34 late-stage deals (Develop / Prove / Agree) had either one contact or no identified buyer. Roughly $5.5M of open ARR sat in structurally thin deals.

The interesting question wasn't "is the pipeline unhealthy" — the data settled that in an afternoon. It was: what definition of "qualified" can survive a CRO and board challenge? That required testing candidate signals against closed outcomes, not opinions.

02

Method: Let Closed Deals Vote

Training set. 168 closed new-logo deals over twelve months — 37 wins, a 22.0% baseline win rate. Every candidate gate signal was tested for won-vs-lost separation (Fisher exact tests for the binary gates; a transparent points rubric benchmarked against a cross-validated logistic regression for the score).

Motion split first. Before testing anything, I separated deal motions — and this reframed the whole project. Expansion deals won at 78%; new logo at 31%. Contact-structure gates tested at p=1.00 on expansion — statistically meaningless there. A universal gate would have throttled the healthiest revenue motion in the business to fix a problem it didn't have.

Design constraint: no black box. The score had to be a transparent points rubric where every point traces to a measured win rate. An AE should be able to see exactly what to fix; a CRO should be able to audit any number. The rubric ultimately outperformed the regression it was benchmarked against — interpretability cost nothing.

Scope decision. The gate applies to new logo only. Expansion is explicitly exempted, pending customer-health signals that actually predict expansion outcomes. Governance that ignores motion physics isn't governance — it's friction.

The Gate That Survived

A new-logo deal is qualified only if it is multi-threaded (≥ 2 contacts) AND has buying authority present — at least one contact at VP / C-level, or explicitly tagged Economic Buyer.
VALIDATED: +23pt won-vs-lost pass-rate separation · 0% of lost deals passed · structural, so it survives CRM hygiene problems

Deployment Design: A Gate People Can Live With

A statistically valid gate that sales refuses to operate is a failed project. The rollout design mattered as much as the math.

Two-track by motion

The gate governs new logo only. Expansion — winning at 78% with no contact-structure signal — is exempt, pending customer-health signals fit for that motion. One-size-fits-all governance was the first thing the data ruled out.

Time-boxed provisional status

Deals that fail the gate aren't deleted — they enter a provisional state with a clock. Reps get a defined window to multi-thread and reach authority before the deal is recycled. The gate coaches before it cuts.

Leadership override, logged

Sales leadership can override the gate on any deal — explicitly and on the record. Overrides become their own dataset: if overridden deals win, the model learns; if they don't, the debate ends itself.

Pre-flight data check

Flagged before rollout: absence of logged contacts is not absence of relationships. Before cutting any "unqualified" deal, verify reps have actually recorded their contacts — otherwise the gate punishes CRM hygiene, not deal quality.

08

What This Demonstrates

The deliverable was a gate. The work was epistemics: refusing to encode any qualification belief — including my own and leadership's — without testing it against closed outcomes first. Half the incumbent assumptions failed. The model that shipped is small, transparent, auditable, and stronger than the black-box benchmark, precisely because everything that couldn't earn its place was cut.
