What has to be true for the business to reach $20M? Everything below exists to answer that question. A revenue target is not a plan — a plan says where the revenue comes from, what has to happen, when it lands, and how much of it rests on assumptions not yet realized.
01

The Situation

Case study

In 2025 the company needed more than a traditional sales forecast. Revenue existed across recognized business, contracted programs awaiting execution, member-based program economics, and commercial pipeline. I built an operating model that connected these states and later expanded it into the foundation for 2026 annual planning.

The 2026 planning question was straightforward but difficult: could a business projected to finish 2025 at $10.6M credibly support a $20M revenue expectation?

Historically, planning had relied heavily on prior-year performance and management judgment. The new model allowed leadership to test the Board-level expectation against the actual mechanics of the business — and to separate three things that usually get blurred together:

Mathematical possibility

The assumptions can theoretically sum to the target.

Operational credibility

The business can reasonably execute those assumptions on time.

Confidence-weighted expectation

The target accounts for uncertainty and execution risk.

06

Member-Based Program Revenue

Contracted ≠ fixed
Signed did not always mean fixed. For member-based programs, final revenue depended on actual program population. The model therefore had to account for post-contract volume variance as well as traditional sales-pipeline uncertainty. Customer Operations typically received the member eligibility file within about two weeks of program start; until then the model planned on expected membership.

Revenue per member is annualized and recognized ratably from program start through December. Programs whose file has not been received use expected members × the active member-variance scenario and are flagged Subject to member reconciliation.

Acquisition Funnel Model

Marketing-sourced baseline demand

These funnels represent baseline marketing-sourced demand. Trade shows, webinars and outbound are modeled separately as incremental motions so they are not counted twice. Opportunities are created evenly through the year; a deal only counts in 2026 if it goes live (cycle + implementation) by December, and then only for its remaining months.

Three Types of Uncertainty

Where the model applies them

Commercial uncertainty

Will the organization win the business?

  • Pipeline conversion
  • Win rate
  • Deal size

Execution uncertainty

Will the business execute the initiative on schedule?

  • Hiring and ramp
  • Product and program launch
  • Customer implementation

Economic uncertainty

Once executed, what will the final value actually be?

  • Actual member population
  • Revenue per member
  • Adoption and expansion value

Finance remained the financial system of record. HubSpot was the operating revenue model and commercial data layer — it did not replace the accounting ledger. Recognized revenue was reconciled with Finance during the first week of each month and written back into the HubSpot revenue objects.

37

Before and After

What changed in how planning worked

Before

Annual expectations were heavily informed by prior-year performance and separate functional assumptions.

Revenue states were difficult to evaluate together, and contracted revenue could change based on customer execution and member population.

Commercial, operational, product and marketing assumptions were not represented in one integrated planning environment.

After

Leadership could evaluate the forward plan using a single connected model:

  • Existing revenue
  • Pending revenue
  • Member-based economics
  • Pipeline
  • Funnel conversion
  • Market expansion
  • Marketing contribution
  • Seller capacity
  • Expansion
  • Product / program launches
  • Timing
  • Confidence
  • Scenario analysis
Annual planning is not a spreadsheet exercise. It is the operating architecture that connects Finance, Sales, Marketing, Product, Customer Operations, customer economics, pipeline, capacity, execution timing and uncertainty into one revenue model that leadership can reason about — and then monitor against, month by month.
