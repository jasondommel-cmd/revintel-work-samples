The wrong instinct

"We scored the sales pipeline — just run the same model on renewal opps."

The design thesis

New-logo closing measures buying behavior. Renewal measures value realization, switching cost, and relationship health. Different physics — different signals, built from scratch.
01

Why Not Reuse the Sales Score

The sales-side scoring work had just proven that qualification signals must be earned per motion — expansion and new logo behaved so differently (78% vs. 31% win rates) that a shared gate was statistically indefensible. Renewals are a third motion again, with their own physics: the customer already bought, already deployed, already has switching costs and a relationship history.

So the first design decision was a refusal: no signal enters this model by analogy to the sales score. Committee coverage, deal age, close-date pushes — the signals that dominated new-logo prediction — may mean something entirely different on a renewal, or nothing at all.

The second decision was sequencing. Before designing a single weight, I ran discovery against the CS organization's actual process documentation and systems — and the documents answered most of the discovery question list before a meeting was needed. What remained was the small set only practitioners could answer: which signals CSMs actually trust, what they wish they could see but can't, and what informal health checks exist outside the documented process.

A scoring model designed before auditing what data actually exists is a slide, not a model. The sales-side build made that lesson expensive enough to remember.

03

Design Decision: The Target Variable Isn't Binary

Filter · click any outcome

"Did they renew — yes or no?" collapses outcomes that mean completely different things to the ARR waterfall. A customer renewing at 60% of prior ACV is not a retained customer with an asterisk; it's a contraction event. The revenue taxonomy I'd built separately already defined the outcome space, so the model targets it directly:

Honest complication, flagged in design: contraction had no opportunity type in the historical CRM data, so the training set for downgrade outcomes may not exist yet. The taxonomy has to be enforced going forward before those classes become learnable — the model roadmap and the data-architecture roadmap are the same roadmap.

04

Candidate Signal Architecture

Filter by evidence status

Four families, twelve candidate signals. Weights are deliberately unassigned — on the sales side, every weight was anchored to a measured win rate, and renewals get the same standard. Each signal carries its evidence status: what's instrumented today, what's a documented operating heuristic, what's a hypothesis, and where coverage itself is unverified.

Show:

Deployment & Adoption

Weight: to be calibrated
  • Deployment % vs. licensed capacityINSTRUMENTEDOperational anchor exists: 80% deployment is the documented implementation-to-maintenance handoff threshold. A customer who never fully deployed is paying for shelf-ware — the cleanest churn hypothesis in the set.
  • Implementation completeness at handoffINSTRUMENTEDWas the account maintenance-ready, or handed off with open items? Design bet: incomplete implementations age into renewal risk.
  • Pending integration / change activityHYPOTHESISOpen platform changes at renewal time cut both ways — investment signal or unresolved friction. Needs testing, not assuming.

Commercial

Weight: to be calibrated
  • Invoice payment statusHEURISTICDocumented CS heuristic: a paid current-term invoice supports a near-certain renewal forecast. Strong prior — but a heuristic is a hypothesis until backtested.
  • Price-increase magnitude vs. account risk tierINSTRUMENTEDThe increase is set at T−145 to T−105 — meaning the score can inform pricing posture, not just predict around it. This is where the model earns money, not just accuracy.
  • ACV trajectory across prior termsHYPOTHESISGrew, held, or shrank at last renewal — prior-term behavior as base rate.

Engagement & Sentiment

Weight: to be calibrated
  • CSM structured evaluationINSTRUMENTEDTwo instrumented inputs exist: an "ARR likely to renew" percentage and a likelihood-to-renew dropdown. Test CSM judgment against outcomes — calibrate it, don't worship or dismiss it.
  • CTA responsiveness after T−75HYPOTHESISDoes the customer engage when structured outreach begins? Silence is a signal.
  • Call-based sentiment signalsUNVERIFIEDCoverage caveat flagged in design: if renewal conversations happen in tickets and email rather than recorded calls, conversation analytics is noise for this motion. Verify coverage before building trackers.

Structural Risk Events

Weight: to be calibrated
  • Churn-request workflow triggersINSTRUMENTEDA formal churn/downgrade request object exists with a four-status workflow through CS and Finance. Any entry into that workflow is a hard, dated risk event — the closest thing this model has to ground truth before the outcome.
  • Quote slippage past T−60INSTRUMENTEDThe process defines the deadline; the model measures the miss.
  • Support tier & escalation activityHYPOTHESISEscalation density in the run-up window, normalized by account tier.
05

Discipline Imported from the Sales-Side Build

The new-logo gate project generated rules the hard way. This model inherits them as constraints, not suggestions:

Audit field population before designing around a field
On the sales side, the "Decision Maker" tag looked authoritative and was populated on 8 of 170 deals. Every candidate renewal signal gets a null-rate audit before it earns a weight.
Populated is not accurate
A filled field proves someone typed something. CSM evaluations and health inputs get validated against outcomes, not taken at face value.
Hunt for documentation bias
Sales-methodology fields were anti-predictive because losses got post-mortems and wins skipped paperwork. The renewal equivalent is plausible — churning accounts may be documented more richly than quiet renewals. Test for it explicitly.
Ban target leakage
Late-lifecycle fields that only fill once the outcome is effectively known (quote signed, churn request resolved) are outcomes wearing a costume. They're excluded from the predictive score and used only as state.
One motion, one model
The lesson that started this project: expansion, new logo, and renewal are different physics. No cross-motion signal borrowing without independent validation.
06

Calibration & Validation Plan

1 · Backtest against closed renewals

Score historical renewal opportunities blind to outcome, then compare score bands to actual results — same protocol that produced the sales rubric's 0.843 AUC. Anchor every weight to a measured retention rate.

2 · Transparent rubric over black box

Points-based and auditable, benchmarked against a regression. A CSM should see exactly which signal moved an account's score; CS leadership should be able to defend any number in a portfolio review.

3 · Lead-time scoring, not point-in-time

Evaluate accuracy at T−145, T−90, and T−60 separately — the score's job is to be right when decisions are made, so it's graded at each decision point, not just at the finish line.

4 · Recalibrate on a cadence

Outcome taxonomy enforcement will improve the training data every quarter. Weights get re-anchored as the contraction and partial-renewal classes become learnable, with each version stamped and dated.

Where it stood: signal architecture and target design complete, discovery narrowed to practitioner-only questions, calibration gated on the outcome-taxonomy enforcement that makes the historical data trustworthy. Shipping an uncalibrated score with invented weights was available at any time — and declined.

07

What This Demonstrates

Most scoring models fail before math is involved — they're designed by analogy, on unaudited data, against a target variable that doesn't match how revenue actually behaves. This framework shows the opposite habit: motion-specific design, a taxonomy-grade target variable, signals sourced from the operating process rather than a template, and a refusal to assign weights the data hasn't earned. The thought process is the deliverable.
